# BuildGenie Labs — Enquiry to Delivery Playbook

Internal operating guide for handling enquiries created through the Project Advisor, email and direct introductions.

## 1. Lead intake

The website currently prepares an email draft. It does not submit or store a lead automatically. A lead only enters the team's workflow after the visitor sends the email or contacts BuildGenie directly.

When an enquiry arrives, record:
- Contact name and email.
- Company, product and website, if supplied.
- Original problem or opportunity.
- Business stage and desired outcome.
- Advisor conversation and initial recommendation, if included.
- Services or engagement the prospect is considering.
- Date received, owner and next action.

## 2. First review

Assign an owner and classify the enquiry:

| Enquiry type | Initial qualification |
|---|---|
| Validation | What assumption or decision needs evidence? |
| Strategy | What business, audience or market decision needs clarity? |
| Marketing & growth | What is the current baseline, target audience and acquisition bottleneck? |
| Technology & products | What user problem, product scope and technical constraints are involved? |
| Specialist services | What exact deliverable, volume and deadline are required? |
| Customer support | What channels, coverage, volume and escalation needs exist? |

Do not assume the advisor recommendation is a final scope. Treat it as a conversation starter.

## 3. Discovery conversation

Before preparing a proposal, clarify:
1. The desired business outcome.
2. The customer or user who needs to benefit.
3. Current stage, evidence and what has already been tried.
4. Scope, dependencies, stakeholders and decision-maker.
5. Timing, budget comfort and constraints.
6. How the client will recognise useful progress.

Keep the discussion focused on the problem before prescribing an engagement.

## 4. Proposal structure

Every proposal should include:
- Brief understanding of the business and challenge.
- Desired outcome and success measures.
- Recommended engagement and rationale.
- Scope, deliverables and exclusions.
- Timeline, milestones, client responsibilities and review rounds.
- Fee, payment schedule and treatment of third-party costs.
- Assumptions, validity period and acceptance step.

Use the published service catalogue as the guide-rate source. Technology work remains custom-scoped. Do not turn a guide rate into a fixed quote where the volume or requirements are not yet defined.

## 5. Commercial terms

Current published terms:
- One-off engagements: 70% to start and 30% before final handover.
- Growth subscriptions: three-month initial term, billed monthly in advance.
- Prepayment discounts: 5% for quarterly prepayment and 10% for annual prepayment.
- Advertising spend, paid tools, creator fees, incentives and other third-party costs are separate and approved before commitment.
- Acquisition targets are agreed from the starting baseline and channel economics; they are not unconditional guarantees.

Always confirm the current rate card and exact scope before sending a proposal.

## 6. Kickoff checklist

Do not begin delivery until the following are agreed:
- Proposal and scope approved.
- Payment milestone received.
- Primary contact and decision-maker confirmed.
- Communication channel and meeting cadence agreed.
- Access, assets and source materials collected securely.
- Feedback owner and approval turnaround agreed.
- Baseline, milestones and reporting format documented.
- First working session scheduled.

## 7. Close the loop

For every enquiry, record the outcome: discovery booked, proposal sent, won, lost, deferred or not a fit. Note the reason and the next date/action where applicable. This creates a useful pipeline without overstating conversion performance.
